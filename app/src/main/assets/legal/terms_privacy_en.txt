81.2. Permission must not be obtained deceptively, repurposed, or retained after
revocation. A website decision and Android's system permission are separate choices and
must not be merged into an ambiguous control.

81.3. Accessibility Service, VPN, Device Administrator, certificate management, local
proxy, root, and similar powers require particularly clear justification. They must not
be added merely for analytics, advertising, retention, or covert traffic control.

81.4. A fork developer should review exported Activities, Services, Receivers,
Providers, deep links, and PendingIntents, prevent unintended cross-app access, and
apply the narrowest file and IPC privileges compatible with the feature.

82. Telemetry, advertising, and data in forks

82.1. Adding analytics, crash reporting, ad SDKs, sync, a search proxy, push services,
or remote configuration gives the fork a data model different from the official build.
Its operator must independently determine legal basis, party roles, retention,
security measures, and mechanisms for individual rights.

82.2. History, queries, URLs, page titles, identifiers, advertising profiles, precise
location, or form content must not be hidden behind "product improvement." A notice
must identify categories, purposes, recipients, transfer countries, retention, and
opt-out methods with the specificity required by law.

82.3. Password fields, payment fields, private messages, or page content must not be
transmitted without a separate lawful basis and strict necessity. Consent is not freely
given when refusal unjustifiably removes a core function or the UI manipulates choice.

82.4. A fork must not copy the official statement "we do not collect history" after
adding a relevant channel. Its distributor is responsible for its own representations;
the original Developers do not inspect its servers or answer requests for data they lack.

83. Signing, updates, and supply-chain security

83.1. An update delivers new executable code and must be protected at least as carefully
as initial installation. An update channel should use modern TLS, verifiable signatures,
protected version metadata, and safeguards against rollback to a known-vulnerable build.

83.2. Arbitrary executable code must not be fetched from a mutable server without a
clear feature, integrity verification, and lawful choice. A fork must not bypass Android
installation confirmation or disguise an APK as media, a document, or a mandatory
"security component."

83.3. Private keys must not be kept in a public repository, source archive, APK, shared
chat, or exposed CI variable. Suspected compromise requires pausing release, investigation,
user warning, and safe migration rather than concealing a certificate change.

83.4. Dependencies and build tools should come from verifiable sources. Version pins,
checksums, least-privilege CI, release logs, and independent review of critical changes
are basic supply-chain hygiene, not a guarantee against compromise.

84. Testing and preserving security properties

84.1. Before distribution, a fork should test clean installation, updates, HTTP/HTTPS
navigation, certificate errors, Safe Browsing, local blocklists, downloads, permissions,
data clearing, tab recovery, and renderer-process failure.

84.2. Changing the engine, targetSdk, minSdk, network configuration, or dependencies
requires a renewed threat assessment. Successful compilation does not prove security,
compatibility, accessibility, store compliance, or absence of regressions.

84.3. Disabled protection must be visible. A fork must not retain a green indicator,
"protected" label, or old description after accepting invalid certificates, allowing
mixed content, enabling debugging, removing tracker blocking, or ceasing feed updates.

84.4. Antivirus, static-analysis, or app-store review is useful but not absolute proof.
A distributor must not promise "100% secure," "phishing impossible," or "fully anonymous"
because one check passed.

85. Fork distribution, support, and incident response

85.1. A fork download page should state publisher, version, date, supported Android
versions, architecture, checksum, signing fingerprint, source link, licenses, privacy
notice, and a reachable vulnerability-reporting channel.

85.2. An APK must not be delivered through a fake system warning, fake official update,
compromised account, spam, malicious ad, or substituted QR code. A person must understand
that a third-party build is being installed.

85.3. On a credible report of a critical vulnerability, key leak, hidden collection, or
malicious dependency, the fork operator should preserve evidence, limit distribution,
identify affected releases, prepare a fix, and notify people as law and reasonable
security require.

85.4. The official Developers may decline to list, promote with their marks, or support
a fork. Removing an official link does not cancel Apache License 2.0 code rights and
does not create a duty to investigate in place of the fork's actual operator.

infrastructure. It does not mean the Internet works without network transfers: a

source, or external app may receive data needed or represented for its own service.

86.3. The Developers do not sell or rent browsing history that the official build does
not send them. They also cannot export, correct, or erase records held by a Website or

86.4. If a future official build adds first-party sync, telemetry, or a server feature,
this description must be updated before that processing begins, and separate consent

87. Data stored locally on the device

87.1. The App may keep settings, the accepted terms revision, restorable tabs,
bookmarks, selected shortcuts, a blocklist, appearance choices, download identifiers,
local threat hashes, and normal engine data in Android private storage.

87.2. Cookies, cache, DOM storage, and other Website data are created by the engine to
operate pages. Weglet restricts third-party cookies and offers clearing controls, but
actual support and deletion timing also depend on WebView or Gecko, Android, and the site.

87.3. "Local" does not mean inaccessible after physical unlocking, malware, root,

87.4. Uninstalling or clearing generally removes data controlled by the App, but it

information independently retained by another party.

88. Protection counters and local HTML analysis

88.1. The blocked-tracker request count, last matched tracker domain, and counts of
HTML checks, warnings, and blocks exist only in process memory for the current session.
The official build neither sends these counters to the Developers nor records them as
a persistent browsing history.

88.2. WebView's local anti-phishing analysis runs after an HTTPS page loads. It checks
for a password form, its action protocol and origin, limited heading and control text,
brand signals in images, and meta refresh.

88.3. The analysis does not read entered input values, intercept a password, capture
the page, or send HTML or results to a Weglet server. It uses structural signals within
the WebView process and returns only a small result code: normal, warning, or block reason.

88.4. This function is available in system WebView mode. Gecko mode uses Content
Blocking, Safe Browsing, and URL checks, but the WebView counter and local DOM analysis
do not describe all Gecko actions and are not a complete log.

88.5. Structural similarity is heuristic. It can miss a new imitation or warn about
legitimate federated authentication. A result is not an accusation, forensic opinion,
or guarantee; a user should independently verify the domain before entering a secret.

89. Search providers and search queries

89.1. When input is not a URL, Weglet constructs a location for the selected search
provider. The query is sent to that provider in a URL or its expected mechanism.
Without that transfer, an independent provider cannot return search results.

89.2. A provider may see the query, IP address, time, protocol headers, cookies,
approximate location, and other data and may associate it with its own account.
Purposes, retention, advertising, and international transfers are determined by that
provider and law applicable to it, not by the Weglet Developers.

89.3. Selecting a provider does not endorse its policy. A user can select another
available provider or a valid custom template but must verify the template endpoint;
a malicious template can send queries to an unexpected third party.

89.4. Clearing local data or changing provider does not send the former provider an
erasure request. Rights concerning its records must be exercised through its contacts
and procedures where applicable.

90. Websites as independent recipients and operators

90.1. Opening a page connects the device to the Website and required hosts. They may
receive IP address, User-Agent, language, time, requested path, referrer, cookies,
device characteristics, and information the user intentionally enters or uploads.

90.2. A Website selects its content, forms, scripts, login, payments, retention, and
recipients. Displaying it does not make Weglet its owner, seller, employer, bank,
physician, or controller of the Website's database.

90.3. Blocking known trackers reduces some requests but cannot prevent first-party
analytics, server-side tracking, fingerprinting, CNAME cloaking, required network
logs, or unknown domains. Protection mode is not anonymity.

90.4. When a Website asks for identity, contact, document, payment, or health data, the
user should read its notice and assess necessity. This document does not replace an
independent Website policy or appoint the Developers as that Website's representative.

91. Networks, DNS, operating system, and device provider

91.1. An ISP, Wi-Fi operator, VPN, DNS resolver, device manufacturer, Android component,
or enterprise administrator may process network metadata within its technical role and
policy. Weglet does not operate their logs or choose their laws.

91.2. HTTPS protects content against ordinary observation between the device and
endpoint, but does not hide every fact of communication from every participant or make
the endpoint honest. HTTP lacks comparable protection and therefore receives a warning
and restricted mode.

91.3. A user-selected VPN or installed root certificate changes routing and trust.
The Developers do not audit a chosen VPN, MDM, antivirus HTTPS filter, or local proxy
and do not promise that it cannot observe traffic.

91.4. A compulsory government request to an independent provider is governed by that
provider's control and applicable law. Data absent from the Weglet Developers may still
exist with a Website, network, platform, or the user.

92. Safe Browsing, Gecko, OpenPhish, and security providers

92.1. WebView uses the system Safe Browsing mechanism, while Gecko uses protections
implemented by that engine. An implementation may download lists, contact its provider,
or otherwise process information according to engine version, documentation, and policy.

92.2. The official build uses OpenPhish as a downloaded indicator feed. An update
request exposes ordinary connection data, including IP address and time, to the feed
host. URLs opened by the user are then matched against local hashes and are not sent
to OpenPhish by Weglet's matching mechanism.

92.3. A feed provider may change availability, format, completeness, or terms. No match
does not prove safety, and a match can be stale or mistaken. The Developers may replace,
disable, or adjust a source to protect users.

92.4. The engine library, Safe Browsing, and threat feed are independent components.
Mention does not create a joint company or transfer the Developers' responsibilities.
Their own licenses, notices, and policies apply to their activities.

93. Downloads, external applications, and media services

93.1. A download transfers a file from a Website through Android DownloadManager to
selected storage. Android and the file provider may process network and file metadata.
Weglet gives warnings but does not perform a complete antivirus examination.

93.2. Share actions, external schemes, APK installation, and file opening transfer data
to the selected application by user choice and Android rules. Weglet does not control
copying, cloud upload, or further use after transfer.

93.3. Search or playback through Audius or another external media service creates
direct requests to that service and its hosts. It may receive the query, IP address,
and technical data under its policy. Weglet does not host its catalog or guarantee
rights in a track.

93.4. Even over valid HTTPS, a file can contain malicious code, a deceptive document,
or unlawful material. The user should verify source, extension, signature, and legality
before opening or sharing.

94. Jurisdictions, cross-border transfers, and independent control

PART XIV. DETAILED SECURITY MODEL AND LIMITS

96. Layered protection without an absolute guarantee

96.1. Weglet combines scheme and domain checks, a local reputation feed, Safe Browsing,
hard blocking of certificate errors, HTTP warnings, mixed-content restrictions,
engine anti-tracking, a user blocklist, and local form analysis in WebView.

96.2. The layers complement each other because no domain heuristic, list, or HTML
analysis detects every new threat. An unknown domain can become malicious immediately,
and a compromised legitimate Website may still have valid TLS.

96.3. "Protection," "blocked," and the TLS mark describe a particular completed check,
not a promise about the whole page, transaction, person, file, or next link. A user
should not disclose a secret solely because an indicator has a reassuring color.

96.4. The Developers may adjust heuristics to reduce harm and false alerts. A threshold
change is not an admission that an earlier release was negligent; quality depends on
actual circumstances and knowledge reasonably available at the time.

97. Tracker protection and the session report

97.1. Weglet compares resource domains against a local set of known advertising and
analytics hosts, blocks matches, and displays the request count in the Protection
Center. Subdomains of listed roots are matched as well.

97.2. The count includes only requests observed by Weglet's own WebView interceptor in
the current process. It may omit earlier engine blocks, Gecko requests, cached resources,
or server-side tracking and is not a complete list of companies attempting processing.

97.3. The last domain is shown for explainability, not as a public accusation. One host
can serve multiple functions, and its presence does not prove unlawful conduct by the
Website owner. The list is a defensive product rule.

97.4. Blocking can break a widget, video, login, payment, or analytics required by a
site. The absence of a per-site exception in a release does not create a right to
compatibility with every third-party script.

98. URL anti-phishing and actual-domain checks

98.1. Before navigation, Weglet analyzes length, control and bidirectional characters,
userinfo, backslashes, IP addresses, punycode, mixed scripts, subdomain count, unusual
ports, shorteners, sensitive words, and imitation of supported brands.

98.2. A normalized domain is shown where useful. A path, subdomain, and text before
`@` are not the registrable domain owner. A person must inspect the actual root and
account for country-code domains.

98.3. High risk can be blocked without bypass, while ambiguous risk can show a warning.
This safety choice does not allege that every blocked operator committed a crime or
that every allowed address received human review.

98.4. User approval of a suspicious address concerns that navigation only and does not
make a Website permanently trusted. Redirects, domain changes, and new sessions can
trigger checks again.

99. Structural form and page-similarity analysis

99.1. On an HTTPS WebView page, a local script first looks for a password-type field.
When one exists, it checks the form action and a limited set of structural signals.
The entered field value is never requested or used.

99.2. A password submission to HTTP is blocked immediately. Another high-confidence
signal occurs when login structure, headings, and image cues collectively imitate a
supported known brand while the actual domain is not one of that brand's roots.

99.3. A cross-origin HTTPS action or meta refresh near a login form produces a warning
because legitimate SSO and payment flows can redirect too. The user should verify both
domains and stop when a recipient is unexpected.

99.4. This is structural-similarity analysis, not page copying, biometric brand
identification, or a cloud neural network. It does not compare every pixel or retain
reference HTML, so an attacker can evade it through changes.

99.5. A Website can create a form after the check or submit through script without a
standard action. Tracker blocking and mixed-content restrictions reduce risks but do
not replace password managers, hardware keys, multifactor authentication, and caution.

100. HTTP warning and restricted mode

100.1. A main navigation to `http://` is not opened silently. The user is told that
the connection is unencrypted and can be read or changed by the network and may decline
or explicitly open that exact address once.

100.2. For a one-time opening, Weglet disables JavaScript and DOM storage for the
insecure page and continues blocking unapproved HTTP resources. This reduces attack
surface but does not encrypt the connection or make entered data confidential.

100.3. Approval applies only to that URL in the current process, not the entire domain.
A redirect or new session may require another decision, and the site may not work fully
without JavaScript.

100.4. Passwords, payment, health, work, or identity data should not be entered through
HTTP. A private network, familiar router name, or absence of visible attack does not
provide cryptographic integrity.

101. TLS, certificates, and mixed content

101.1. On a TLS error, Weglet cancels loading and provides no ordinary "continue"
button. An expired, untrusted, wrong-domain, or otherwise invalid certificate may
indicate site misconfiguration, interception, or an incorrect clock.

101.2. No bypass is an intentional barrier. A user may correct time or network settings
or contact the site owner, but should not install an unknown root certificate merely
to remove the warning.

101.3. WebView denies mixed active content on HTTPS. A site trying to load an HTTP
script, image, or tracker may render incompletely. Compatibility does not take priority
over a hidden security downgrade.

101.4. A valid certificate confirms a cryptographic connection to the domain under the
trust infrastructure. It does not prove seller honesty, product quality, absence of
phishing on a compromised domain, or legality of content.

102. False positives, misses, and rule updates

102.1. Reputation lists and heuristics necessarily have false positive and negative
results. A user may report a minimal example but receives no guarantee of immediate
reclassification, permanent availability, or compensation for a block.

102.2. The Developers may add or remove a domain, brand, TLD, structural signal, or feed
provider after quality review. Publishing every detail can assist evasion, so internal
implementation need not be disclosed beyond legal requirements.

102.3. Disabling an available protective feature increases risk. A switch does not mean
the Developers recommend disabling it, while the absence of a switch does not assume
liability for every incompatible Website.

102.4. An old release may lack new rules and engine fixes. Users should obtain APKs from
a verified channel, check signatures, and update Android System WebView, relevant Chrome
components, Gecko dependencies, and the OS within device support.

103. Practical user security responsibilities

103.1. Before disclosing a secret, verify the full domain, TLS, link source, form purpose,
and whether the request was expected. Urgency, account-lock threats, prizes, and requests
for one-time codes are reasons to stop and verify another channel.

103.2. Use unique passwords, a password manager, multifactor protection, and separate
confirmation of financial transactions. SMS, push, or hardware-key codes must not be
given to a person who unexpectedly requests them.

103.3. Verify APKs and executable files by source, signature, and checksum. Weglet does
not automatically execute a download, but opening it in another application can give
code that application's or the system's capabilities.

103.4. After suspected phishing, close the page, do not submit, change an entered
password from a trusted device, revoke sessions, contact the bank or service, and check
the device. A project report does not replace urgent account-owner action.
